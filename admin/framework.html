The Well Tough AI Adoption Framework

A repeatable, evidence-led system for introducing AI into any small or mid-sized organisation. It begins with business value, protects people and data, and only scales what has proved a return.

North-star rule

Outcome before tool. Workflow before prompt. Evidence before scale.

Every initiative must have an owner, baseline, target, review control, adoption plan and stop/scale decision.

Operating doctrine

Ten principles that protect ROI

Use these as non-negotiable tests in every proposal, workshop, pilot and steering meeting.

01

Start with friction

Find expensive delays, rework, queues, errors and knowledge bottlenecks before discussing products.

02

Baseline first

Measure time, volume, quality, cost and experience before changing the workflow.

03

Augment deliberately

Keep judgement, accountability and sensitive decisions with people; automate repeatable cognitive work.

04

Use the lightest stack

Exploit secure capabilities in existing systems before adding licences, integrations or custom builds.

05

Redesign the workflow

A faster task inside a broken process rarely produces a meaningful business return.

06

Design controls in

Data rules, human review, audit evidence and exception handling belong in the workflow, not in a later policy.

07

Pilot narrowly

Use a representative team, bounded data and one measurable outcome before wider deployment.

08

Train by role

People learn through their real tasks, examples and decisions—not generic demonstrations.

09

Count realised value

Saved minutes are not ROI until capacity is removed, redeployed, converted into revenue or used to improve service.

10

Stop weak ideas

Every pilot needs explicit stop, improve and scale thresholds to prevent sunk-cost projects.

Eight-stage lifecycle

From business intent to continuous improvement

Each stage produces evidence for a decision gate. Do not advance because activity occurred; advance because the evidence is good enough.

1
AlignDefine ambition, ownership and economic boundaries
Gate: mandate

Questions

  • Which strategic outcomes matter in the next 12–24 months?
  • Where is margin, growth, capacity, service or risk under pressure?
  • Who sponsors AI, owns delivery and can remove blockers?
  • What investment, staff time and risk appetite are available?

Activities

  • CEO/owner and leadership interviews.
  • Business model, financial driver and constraint review.
  • AI ambition and change narrative workshop.
  • Stakeholder, decision-right and capacity mapping.

Outputs

  • One-page AI mandate.
  • Named executive sponsor and programme lead.
  • Value themes and investment guardrails.
  • Initial stakeholder and communications map.

Exit evidence

  • Leadership agrees why AI matters now.
  • Success is expressed as business outcomes.
  • Owners, cadence, budget range and scope are confirmed.
  • No unresolved strategic objection blocks discovery.
2
DiagnoseEstablish readiness, risk and current-state evidence
Gate: truth

Questions

  • How mature are leadership, data, process, people and governance?
  • Where is shadow AI already creating value or risk?
  • Which systems, data and workflows are business-critical?
  • What prevents adoption today?

Activities

  • Complete the AIRI assessment with evidence.
  • Interview leaders, managers, frontline users and IT/support partners.
  • Inventory systems, licences, data classes and current AI use.
  • Capture baseline sentiment, skills and governance gaps.

Outputs

  • AIRI score and maturity heatmap.
  • Current-state architecture and data map.
  • Shadow-AI and risk register.
  • Readiness constraints and prerequisite backlog.

Exit evidence

  • Scores are supported by examples, not opinion alone.
  • Material security, legal or data blockers are visible.
  • Baseline evidence is sufficient to prioritise discovery.
  • Leadership accepts the current-state diagnosis.
3
DiscoverMap workflows and build the opportunity portfolio
Gate: candidates

Questions

  • Where do people wait, search, copy, reconcile, draft or repeat?
  • Which tasks are high-volume, rules-based or knowledge-heavy?
  • Where do errors, inconsistency and slow response affect customers?
  • What work should never be automated?

Activities

  • Run role-based workflow interviews and observation sessions.
  • Map trigger, inputs, steps, systems, decisions, handoffs and exceptions.
  • Quantify frequency, handling time, wait time, error and rework.
  • Generate and cluster opportunities by value theme.

Outputs

  • Current-state process maps.
  • Opportunity backlog with named process owners.
  • Customer/employee pain evidence.
  • Automation boundary and human-judgement map.

Exit evidence

  • Each opportunity points to a real workflow and population.
  • Volume and pain are evidenced.
  • Dependencies and exceptions are understood.
  • Process owners validate the maps.
4
Prioritise & designSelect use cases and design the future workflow
Gate: invest

Questions

  • Which opportunities combine high value, feasibility and adoption readiness?
  • What is the smallest intervention that proves the outcome?
  • Where must humans review, approve or handle exceptions?
  • How will value be captured rather than merely estimated?

Activities

  • Score the portfolio using the Well Tough priority model.
  • Design future-state workflow and control points.
  • Compare existing-stack, bought-tool and built-solution options.
  • Create business case, benefit owner and measurement plan.

Outputs

  • Ranked now/next/later portfolio.
  • Future-state workflow and solution brief.
  • Costed pilot charter and benefits case.
  • Data protection, security and vendor assessments.

Exit evidence

  • Baseline, target and data source exist for every KPI.
  • Benefit owner commits to capturing released value.
  • Controls and exception paths are testable.
  • Sponsor approves time, cost and stop/scale rules.
5
PilotProve workflow, control and human behaviour
Gate: proof

Questions

  • Does the solution work on representative inputs and exceptions?
  • Can users perform the workflow reliably?
  • Are outputs accurate, safe, traceable and useful?
  • Is the measured outcome moving enough to justify scale?

Activities

  • Configure a controlled environment and approved data.
  • Test happy paths, edge cases, adversarial prompts and failure modes.
  • Train the pilot group on real role-based scenarios.
  • Track usage, quality, handling time, incidents and feedback weekly.

Outputs

  • Test log and issue backlog.
  • Working instructions, prompt/workflow assets and SOP.
  • Pilot performance dashboard.
  • Documented user feedback and control evidence.

Exit evidence

  • Minimum usage and quality thresholds are met.
  • No unacceptable unresolved risk remains.
  • Benefits survive total-cost and adoption assumptions.
  • Decision is explicitly stop, improve, extend or scale.
6
AdoptEmbed new behaviour into roles and management routines
Gate: habit

Questions

  • Do affected people understand why, when and how to use the workflow?
  • Are managers reinforcing the expected behaviour?
  • Have workload, role and performance expectations changed?
  • Can users get help and report failures quickly?

Activities

  • Complete stakeholder impact and resistance analysis.
  • Deliver role-based training with observed practice.
  • Activate champions, office hours and a support route.
  • Update SOPs, onboarding, objectives and management reviews.

Outputs

  • Change and communication plan.
  • Role-based learning paths and competency checks.
  • Champion network and support knowledge base.
  • Adoption dashboard by team and use case.

Exit evidence

  • Target users demonstrate competence.
  • Active usage is sustained without project-team prompting.
  • Managers own reinforcement and exceptions.
  • Employee feedback and customer impact remain acceptable.
7
ScaleExtend only what remains valuable and controllable
Gate: repeatability

Questions

  • Will the result hold across teams, volumes, locations and edge cases?
  • Can technology, support and governance handle wider use?
  • What must be standardised and what may be locally adapted?
  • Is marginal benefit greater than marginal scale cost?

Activities

  • Package reusable workflow, controls, training and metrics.
  • Roll out in waves with readiness checks and hypercare.
  • Test capacity, permissions, integration and service support.
  • Track cohort performance and correct variation.

Outputs

  • Scale playbook and wave plan.
  • Production service ownership and support model.
  • Standard controls with permitted local variants.
  • Updated financial forecast and capacity plan.

Exit evidence

  • Later cohorts reproduce the pilot outcome.
  • Support demand and incidents stay within tolerance.
  • Value exceeds recurring and change costs.
  • Business owner accepts operational ownership.
8
Optimise & governSustain value, controls and portfolio choices
Gate: resilience

Questions

  • Is value continuing after novelty and project support end?
  • Have model, vendor, data, regulation or process conditions changed?
  • Which use cases should be improved, retired or replicated?
  • Is realised value being reinvested intelligently?

Activities

  • Run monthly operational and quarterly portfolio reviews.
  • Monitor quality, drift, incidents, adoption, cost and benefit.
  • Retest controls and review vendors, permissions and data.
  • Maintain improvement backlog and benefits ledger.

Outputs

  • Live AI portfolio dashboard.
  • Benefits ledger and risk register.
  • Retirement, remediation and optimisation actions.
  • Updated AIRI score and next-wave roadmap.

Exit evidence

  • Benefits remain evidenced and owned.
  • Controls operate and incidents close on time.
  • Redundant tools and weak use cases are retired.
  • Learning feeds the next diagnostic cycle.

Do now

High value / high readiness

Pilot with clear metrics and a short decision horizon.

Enable next

High value / low readiness

Fix data, process, skills or controls before buying technology.

Quick proof

Moderate value / high readiness

Use only if it builds capability or proves a reusable pattern cheaply.

Do not pursue

Low value / high complexity

Record the reasoning and protect the team from distraction.

Value architecture

Measure ROI from baseline to realised benefit

Use conservative ranges, include the full cost of change, and separate theoretical capacity from banked financial value.

1

Baseline

Annual effort cost = cases × minutes per case ÷ 60 × loaded hourly cost.

Also capture wait time, error, rework, conversion, customer outcome and risk events.

2

Gross benefit

Capacity value = annual hours saved × loaded hourly cost.

Add incremental gross profit, avoided external cost, reduced loss and quantified risk where defensible.

3

Realisation

Realised benefit = gross benefit × adoption rate × sustained-use rate × capture rate.

Capture rate reflects whether capacity is removed, redeployed or converted into throughput.

4

Total cost

Licences + implementation + integration + data work + training + internal time + support + control + expected failure cost.

5

Decision metrics

ROI = (realised benefit − total cost) ÷ total cost × 100.

Payback months = total cost ÷ monthly realised benefit.

6

Confidence

Model downside, expected and upside cases. State assumptions, evidence source, owner and confidence level.

Reforecast at pilot, 30, 60 and 90 days after rollout.

Common ROI traps

  • Counting all saved time at full salary without a plan to redeploy it.
  • Ignoring manager review, correction, integration, training and support time.
  • Using licence activation as evidence of adoption.
  • Measuring output quantity while quality, risk or customer experience worsens.
  • Annualising a short novelty-driven pilot without testing sustained use.
  • Claiming revenue influenced by AI when other changes drove the result.

Proportionate control

Govern by use-case risk, not by fear or enthusiasm

Apply stronger evidence, approval and monitoring as consequence and autonomy increase.

Tier 1

Assistive / low risk

Drafting, summarising and ideation using non-sensitive data with user review.

  • Approved tool and basic training
  • User verifies output
  • No automatic external action
Tier 2

Operational / moderate risk

Internal decisions, customer communications or workflows using business data.

  • Named process owner
  • Documented testing and human approval
  • Access, logging and incident controls
Tier 3

Material / high risk

Employment, finance, legal, safety, regulated advice, sensitive personal data or consequential decisions.

  • Specialist legal/security review
  • Formal impact assessment
  • Independent validation and continuous monitoring
Stop

Prohibited

Unlawful, deceptive, discriminatory, unreviewable or uncontrolled uses and tools outside approved data protections.

  • Block and document
  • Escalate suspected incidents
  • Offer a safe alternative workflow

Behaviour change

Make the new way easier, safer and more useful

Adoption is a management outcome. Training is one intervention inside a broader system of role design, reinforcement and support.

Explain

Give a credible reason for change, acknowledge uncertainty and define what AI will and will not do.

Evidence:

People can explain the purpose and boundaries in their own words.

Involve

Co-design workflows with process experts, sceptics and affected users—not only enthusiasts.

Evidence:

Design reflects real exceptions and staff see their input acted upon.

Equip

Provide approved access, role-based examples, practice data, checklists and manager guidance.

Evidence:

Users demonstrate tasks and review outputs safely.

Reinforce

Build expected use into SOPs, one-to-ones, team meetings, onboarding and performance conversations.

Evidence:

Usage continues after launch support reduces.

Support

Create champions, office hours, searchable guidance and a fast route for questions and incidents.

Evidence:

Issues are resolved and learning improves the standard workflow.

Recognise

Celebrate verified improvements, safe challenge and shared learning—not raw prompt volume.

Evidence:

Teams share reusable practice and report problems without fear.

Adoption funnel

  1. Eligible users
  2. Access enabled
  3. Training completed
  4. First successful use
  5. Weekly active use
  6. Correct workflow use
  7. Sustained outcome improvement

Leading indicators

  • Training and competency completion
  • Active use by role and workflow
  • Output acceptance/correction rate
  • Support requests and confidence
  • Manager reinforcement cadence

Lagging indicators

  • Cycle time and cost per case
  • Quality, rework and error
  • Customer and employee experience
  • Capacity/revenue actually captured
  • Incidents and control effectiveness

Discovery questions for every SMB

  • How does the business make money and where does margin leak?
  • Which customer promises are hardest to keep?
  • What work depends on one experienced person?
  • Where are queues, handoffs, rekeying and avoidable rework?
  • Which systems are core and which spreadsheets quietly run the business?
  • What data is sensitive, regulated or commercially critical?
  • What has already been tried and why did it stall?
  • What capacity can leadership genuinely protect for change?

First implementation wave

A practical 90-day mobilisation pattern

Adapt to risk and readiness. Complex or regulated cases may need a longer design and assurance period.

Days 1–15

Align & diagnose

  • Confirm sponsor, outcomes and cadence.
  • Run AIRI and stakeholder interviews.
  • Map stack, data, shadow AI and risks.
  • Capture baseline business measures.
Decision:

Is there enough mandate and readiness to proceed?

Days 16–30

Discover & prioritise

  • Map 5–10 material workflows.
  • Build and score opportunity backlog.
  • Select 1–3 controlled pilot candidates.
  • Assign process and benefit owners.
Decision:

Which cases merit design investment?

Days 31–45

Design & assure

  • Design future workflow and controls.
  • Select lightest viable technology.
  • Complete risk/vendor/data reviews.
  • Agree target, cost and stop/scale rules.
Decision:

Is each pilot safe and economically credible?

Days 46–70

Pilot & learn

  • Configure, test and train pilot users.
  • Run real work with monitoring.
  • Review performance weekly.
  • Correct workflow, support and controls.
Decision:

Stop, improve, extend or prepare to scale?

Days 71–90

Embed & decide

  • Validate sustained outcome and ROI.
  • Transfer ownership into operations.
  • Package SOP, training and controls.
  • Approve scale wave and next backlog.
Decision:

What value was proved and what happens next?

Definition of success

AI is adopted only when the business works better without depending on the project team.

The outcome is a safer, faster and more resilient operating model with measurable value—not a collection of licences, prompts or demonstrations.
